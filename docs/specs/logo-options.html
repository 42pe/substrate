The cut plug

Option B, revised. Two changes: the green ramp moved up a step throughout, and the stem now ends in a leaf rather than a bud — a symmetrical two-block tip on a straight stem read badly, and an angled blade fixes it. The blade's direction is fixed rather than following the bend, because letting it flip mid-sway looked like a glitch.

Cut plug — working direction

The same idea at the site's own 2:1 dimetric angle — a plug of earth lifted out, its two strata banding the cut faces and meeting in a V at the near corner, which is what horizontal layers do on an isometric cube.

Why this one might win

It belongs to the same world as the hero. The light rule is the meadow's — upper-left, so the left cheek is lit and the right is in shade, in both layers. Drop it beside the field scene and it looks like one tile lifted out of it.

What it gives up

Detail. Below about 24 px the V-seam and the two cheeks collapse into one brown mass and it stops being legibly layered — check the 16 px sample above before deciding. It is also wider than it is tall, which is awkward in a square favicon slot.

Side-on — superseded

Two flat bands, read straight on. The mark is the seam: a lit lip, topsoil, a dark furrow line, subsoil. Reads at any size and crops to a square without losing the plant.

Why this one might win

It survives 16 px. The soil is two solid bands, so at favicon size you still read earth, with something growing — the silhouette does not depend on any detail smaller than two pixels.

What it gives up

It says nothing about the isometric world the site is built in. Next to the meadow scenes it reads as a different drawing rather than a detail lifted out of one.
